## What the company does

DevCom Digital Marketing Services is a digital company based in Baybay City, Leyte, Philippines. eGuard is its parental control product, built for Filipino families with peso pricing, local payment methods and a privacy policy written under Philippine law. Whether DevCom does any work other than eGuard is [TO CONFIRM: whether DevCom does any work other than eGuard].

eGuard lets parents set screen time, bedtime, app and web protections on their children's phones, tablets and browsers, then shows whether each one is really working. The problem eGuard addresses is that most parental controls stop at "Saved": a setting can be saved and still not be on the device, because the phone was offline, a system update reset a permission, or someone switched it off. eGuard sends each setting to the child's device, and the device reports back what it actually has. Only then is the setting marked Verified; if the device reports something else, the parent sees Failed and what the device reported.

The company's public positioning rests on three points:

- **Verification.** Protections are checked on the device, not assumed. Verification and Configuration Health are on every plan, including Free.
- **Made for the Philippines.** Prices in pesos, payment by GCash, Maya, QR Ph or card, and Philippine context in the free parent guides.
- **Privacy.** eGuard is never hidden on the child's device, collects settings, screen-time totals and app names rather than messages, photos or browsing content, shows no ads and sells no data.

## Customers and markets

DevCom's market is consumer parental control software for families in the Philippines. The eGuard Facebook Page lists its service area as "Philippines". The currency is set out in "Working hours, time zone and currency" below.

| Customer group | Who they are | How they buy |
|---|---|---|
| Families | Parents and guardians aged 18 and over. The subscription belongs to the family, not to one parent | Only the family admin buys or cancels a plan, on the web at www.eguard.family (Settings › Subscription), paid through PayMongo. The apps show the plan but do not sell it |
| Organizations | Schools, community groups and businesses that want to help many families at once, for example a barangay funding plans for families who cannot pay, a school encouraging parents to set up protection, or a company offering eGuard as an employee benefit | An organization admin buys sponsor codes in batches on the website, and families redeem the codes for a paid plan |

Organizations only ever see counts (families joined, codes redeemed), never a family's data.

## How work is requested and approved

The sources describe these ways work comes in and gets approved. Spending and approval limits are not recorded: [TO CONFIRM: who may approve spending, and the limits].

1. **Product, pricing and engineering decisions.** A written recommendation lists the options and a recommended answer, and the owner accepts or rejects each one. Accepted decisions are dated in the document that proposed them. Examples: Windows PCs included in every plan (accepted 2026-10-23) and the plan-limit change (2026-10-28).
2. **Customer requests.** Parents file support tickets from the app's Contact Support or the website's Settings › Support. Staff read and close tickets in the console; replies go out from the support inbox.
3. **Email to many parents.** Staff write a broadcast in the console, send themselves a test, then confirm before sending or scheduling it. Account changes, security notices and outages go as a service notice; news goes as a product update. The terms are defined in the "Company Glossary"; the steps are in the procedure "Sending a Broadcast to Parents".
