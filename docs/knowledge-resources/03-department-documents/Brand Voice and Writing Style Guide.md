# Brand Voice and Writing Style Guide

## Summary

The Brand Voice and Writing Style Guide sets out how DevCom Digital Marketing Services writes about eGuard: who the writing is for, the voice, the words to use and avoid, formatting, the structure of emails and messages, social posts, and the claims that may and may not be made. It applies to everyone who writes for eGuard, including the website, app store listings, the Knowledge Center, the Facebook Page, press material, support replies and console broadcasts. The guiding rule from the sources is to claim only what is true today: no user counts, ratings or "trusted by" lines until they are real, iPhone and iPad described as coming soon, and screenshots that show the demo family only. Examples are quoted from existing eGuard material; no new slogans are introduced here.

## Who eGuard writes for

eGuard writing is for the following readers. Each has its own rules.

| Reader | What the sources say about writing for them |
|---|---|
| Parents and guardians in the Philippines (18 and over) | The main audience. Use Philippine context: pesos, GCash and Maya, local apps, school terms such as "school nights" and "Grade 7". English and Filipino are both used |
| Schools, community groups and businesses | Explain what an organization sees (counts only), how sponsoring works, and give a clear next step. The for-schools page is the reference |
| Press (tech, startup and business, and parenting outlets in the Philippines) | One pitch per editor, under 150 words, with a timely angle |
| App store reviewers | Plain, factual notes; say what the app does with sensitive permissions |
| Children | Only on pages made for them, such as the kids page at www.eguard.family/for-kids and its Filipino version. Never on the Facebook Page or in ads: Meta does not allow Pages or ads directed at children |

Filipino text should be read by a native Filipino speaker before it is promoted; the sources note the Filipino kids page "was written carefully, but not by a native speaker". Button names inside the app stay in English, since that is what the child sees.

## Voice trait: honest and provable

eGuard writes only what it can back up. Parents are deciding whether to trust eGuard with their children's devices, "so anything on the site that isn't true costs that trust". A claim is added "only once you can back it up".

| eGuard writes like this | Not like this |
|---|---|
| "Free for 1 child · No card needed" | "4.8 (2.5K+ reviews)" |
| "Built for Families, Schools and Communities" | "Trusted by modern families" / "Join thousands of parents who trust eGuard" |
| "The iPhone and iPad app is coming soon." | A headline that says "Android and iOS" |
| "Push alerts (coming soon)" while push is not confirmed as working [TO CONFIRM: whether push alerts work] | "Real-time alerts" |

## Voice trait: concrete and specific

eGuard writing names the actual setting, number or screen instead of a feeling. The site review replaced general lines with concrete ones.

| eGuard writes like this | Not like this |
|---|---|
| "Parental controls that prove they're on" | "A Safer Digital World for Their Brighter Tomorrow" |
| "Works on Android, Chrome, Edge and Firefox" | "Peace of mind" |
| "Bedtime: apps lock at 9:30 PM and come back in the morning" | "Set healthy device limits" |
| "Set screen time, bedtime and app rules for your child, and know they're working." | Values copy with no product fact |

The verification difference is the thread through titles and descriptions: "Competitors say a setting was saved; eGuard says it was confirmed on the device." Existing lines that carry it include "Parental controls you can verify" (site title and Facebook bio), "Saved isn't the same as on" (landing page), "Screen time you can verify" (App Store subtitle) and "Protections you set once, verified on every device" (share images).

## Voice trait: useful first

eGuard leads with help, not a pitch. The Knowledge Center rule is "Useful first. No product pitches in the body. The page adds one short eGuard box at the end; that's enough." In parent communities, staff "lead with a useful guide rather than a sales pitch" and mention eGuard "only as the tool that checks the settings". Grooming and exploitation guides keep the reporting resources first and the product quiet.

| eGuard writes like this | Not like this |
|---|---|
| Share a Knowledge Center guide, such as Facebook privacy settings for teens | An ad for the app posted in a parents' group |
| "Not ready? Read the guide for your child's age" | Repeating the same sign-up call to action twice |
| Say what a platform setting does and roughly where it is, and that names may differ | Exact menu paths for third-party apps presented as permanent |

## Voice trait: transparent with the child and respectful of privacy

eGuard is never presented as a way to watch a child secretly. The sources say: "eGuard is never hidden." Store policy also forbids describing it as "spying, secret or hidden".

| eGuard writes like this | Not like this |
|---|---|
| "Will my child know eGuard is on? Yes. eGuard is never hidden." | Describing eGuard as spying, secret or hidden |
| "It measures the devices' settings, never your child's behavior." | Presenting Configuration Health as a judgment of the child |
| "eGuard never sees messages, photos, calls, what your child types, or which websites they visit" | Suggesting eGuard reads messages, photos or browsing content |

## Formatting conventions

eGuard copy follows these formats.

| Item | Convention | Example from the sources |
|---|---|---|
| Product names | "eGuard" with a lower-case "e" and capital "G", also at the start of a sentence | "eGuard helps parents set up…" |
| Plan names | Capitalized as names | Free, eGuard Plus, eGuard Family |
| Status words | Capitalized and often bold | **Verified**, **Failed** |
| Currency | Philippine pesos (₱), symbol before the amount, comma for thousands, centavos only when the amount has them | ₱149, ₱1,490, ₱1,609.20 |
| Price period | "a month" / "a year" in prose; "/month" in tables and short copy | "₱149 a month"; "₱149/month" |
| Numbers | Digits for counts, limits and times | "1 child", "3 days", "15 minutes" |
| Times | 12-hour clock with AM/PM | "9:30 PM" |
| Time zone | Philippine time (Asia/Manila) when a time matters | Scheduled broadcasts use Philippine time |
| Dates in prose | Day, month in words, year | "4 October 2026"; the app shows "Oct 12, 2026" |
| Screen paths | The "›" separator | Settings › Subscription |
| Web addresses | www.eguard.family and its paths | www.eguard.family/learn |

## Email and message structure

eGuard emails and messages follow the patterns already used in the sources.

**Broadcast emails (console).** Plain text only. The greeting "Hi <first name>," and the footer are added automatically; mailing-list emails start "Hi," with no name. A blank line starts a paragraph. Each email has one kind:

- **Product update:** news and new features, only to parents who opted in, with an unsubscribe link.
- **Service notice:** terms or privacy changes, security notices, outages and account changes, to every verified parent. A service notice carries no marketing; it is never used for news.

The plan-change notices of 28 October 2026 show the structure: a short subject that states the outcome ("Your eGuard plan keeps its limits", "Changes to eGuard Free"), "Hi,", what changes and what stays, a short list, a link to the pricing page, and the sign-off "The eGuard team".

**Write with AI in the console.** Drafts never invent features, prices, dates or links. A missing fact comes back as "[TODO: …]", and an email containing it cannot be sent until staff fill it in. Every draft still goes through preview, a test email and confirmation.

**Press pitches.** One email per editor, under 150 words, with the press release pasted below the signature rather than attached. Follow up once, five working days later, with one new detail, then stop.

**Messenger instant reply.** "Hi! Thanks for messaging eGuard. We usually reply within a working day. For help setting up, see https://www.eguard.family/help. To start free, go to https://www.eguard.family/register."

## Social media and public posts

eGuard's Facebook Page is for parents. Rules from the sources:

1. Nothing on the Page speaks to children.
2. The Page name is "eGuard", with no "Official" or keywords; the username is @eguardfamily.
3. The recommended bio is "Parental controls you can verify. Screen time, bedtime and app rules for Filipino families." A Filipino option exists: "Parental controls na sigurado kang gumagana. Para sa mga pamilyang Pilipino. Libre para sa 1 anak."
4. Check prices against www.eguard.family/pricing before posting.
5. Screenshots and photos show the demo family only: no real child's name, photo or location, and the location pin on a public landmark.
6. In parent groups, read the rules and ask the admin before posting.
7. Any sponsored creator post is labeled as sponsored.
8. Check each shared link's preview in the Facebook Sharing Debugger first.

**What needs approval before publishing.** The sources describe checks, not approvers. ## Claims eGuard may not make

eGuard copy may not make these claims.

| Do not claim | Rule from the sources |
|---|---|
| User counts, ratings, reviews, "trusted by" | Not until they are real; no rating in structured data |
| That iPhone or iPad is supported | Coming soon only |
| PC or Windows features | Not until eGuard for Windows ships |
| That a named school uses eGuard | Only after a pilot becomes a real quote |
| Spying, secret or hidden monitoring | Forbidden by store stalkerware policies and against the product |
| Prices, a buy button or "subscribe at our website" in the apps or store listings | Store payment and anti-steering rules |
| Web prices or links to web checkout in the iOS app | App Store rules |
| Testimonials without consent | Parent must agree first |
| Features not built (family-wide policies, advanced recommendations) | Add to a plan card only once they ship |

The Knowledge Center has two further rules: never tell parents to save sexual images of a child, even as evidence; and hedge platform details, because apps rename settings often.

Store listings that mention monitoring must say that only parents or guardians may install eGuard and that the child's device shows that eGuard is active, with a notification whenever it is running.
